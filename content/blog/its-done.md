Remember to Die has a [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3390530/Remember_to_Die/) you can wishlist right now. It isn't released yet. That distinction matters to me, and I wanted to say the quiet part out loud: shipping is still ahead, but the project crossed a line the moment it became something you could save and come back to.

It's a tactical dice roguelike about memory and mortality, with 32 dice, 112 mementos, over a billion possible story combinations, and four secret endings beyond the standard finale. Made by one person over eighteen months, mostly between 11pm and 3am, using AI as a creative instrument and stubbornness as a production methodology. When it launches, it'll be $6.99 USD, and I'm building it to run beautifully on Steam Deck.

That's the pitch. If you've read the previous posts you already know the rest.

A few honest things I learned, in case any of it is useful to someone sitting where I was sitting eighteen months ago.

The tools are genuinely there. If you have a game in your head and you've been waiting for the right moment or the right skills or the right budget: that moment is probably now. AI won't make the creative decisions for you and it won't care about your game the way you do. But it will handle a remarkable amount of the work that previously required either money or years of technical training.

Know what you're good at and stay in your lane. I'm a designer and a writer who has thought about games for forty years. I am not a programmer, an artist, or a composer. The project worked because I was honest about that from the start.

The unglamorous stuff takes longer than the fun stuff. Learning the engine, learning the art tools, navigating platform requirements, sorting out the business side, figuring out marketing: none of it is as interesting as making the game. Do it anyway.

Put the thing where people can find it. The fear of nobody noticing is real and legitimate and does not go away because you hit "publish" on a store page. Wishlists help. If any of this resonated with you, [adding RTD to your wishlist](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3390530/Remember_to_Die/) is the single most practical way to tell Steam's algorithms the game deserves a shot when launch day comes.

And if you're someone who always wanted to make something but kept finding reasons not to: the tools are waiting. Dream big, push through the parts where nothing works, work out what AI is good at and what you're good at, and trust that the strangest concepts sometimes come together in the most unexpected ways.

That last bit I mean genuinely. This game started as a thought about memory and ended up being about dice and mortality and the lives we choose to remember. I didn't see that coming. It came together anyway.
