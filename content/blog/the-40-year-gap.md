My mum rented Kid Chameleon on the Sega Mega Drive when I was a kid. I don't remember how old I was exactly, but I remember the feeling: the desperate wish that I could reach inside the cartridge and make my own levels, my own abilities, my own version of the game.

That feeling never went away. It just got more expensive to ignore.

For four decades it sat in the background, patient and persistent. I played everything I could find. I finished games, abandoned games, fell in love with games. I spent the last several years hunting for something specific: a game dark enough to mean something, mechanically deep enough to keep pulling me back, narratively rich enough to feel like it was actually about something.

I found things that came close. Binding of Isaac got under my skin in a way few games do. Balatro reminded me that elegance and depth aren't opposites. But nothing quite landed where I wanted it to.

So I decided to make it myself. Reasonable response.

I want to be clear about what that decision meant at the time: I am not a developer. I am not an artist. I am not a composer. I had no team, no budget, no publisher, and no realistic path to acquiring any of those things. What I had was forty years of knowing exactly what kind of game I wanted to play, an obsessive streak, and a suspicion that the tools had finally caught up to the dream.

That suspicion turned out to be correct. Mostly.

The subject matter isn't random either. The game came from a real place, from thinking about memory, about the versions of events we carry with us, and what happens when we're not entirely honest with ourselves about which of those versions is true. But that's a story for another post.

Remember to Die is a tactical dice roguelike about memory, mortality, and occasionally doing obscene damage numbers. It's the game I spent four decades looking for. It's finished. It's real. And it's coming to Steam on 26 September.
